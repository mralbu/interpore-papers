About

The International Society for Porous Media (InterPore) has met every year since 2009, and each meeting brings together several hundred talks and posters on flow, transport and mechanics in porous media, from rocks and soils to batteries, filters and living tissue. The abstracts are spread over one conference site per year. This site puts 8,115 of them, from eleven meetings (2016–2026), on one searchable list and one topic map, and tells the history of the meeting since its start.

A personal project, not affiliated with InterPore. The abstracts belong to their authors.

Every step runs locally and is cached, so adding a meeting only processes that meeting's abstracts.

Abstracts, not papers

InterPore meetings collect abstracts only: there are no full papers or proceedings. What is online depends on the year:

2018–2026
Every meeting runs on the society's Indico server, events.interpore.org, whose public export gives each contribution's title, abstract, authors and affiliations, presenter, format, mini-symposium and any slides or posters the authors uploaded.
2016, 2017
Before Indico, the books of abstracts are PDFs on interpore.org. Their text is parsed into the same fields; each entry links to its page in the book.
2009–2015
No abstracts are online. These meetings appear only in the History view.

How the abstracts were organized

  • Each abstract gets an ID from its meeting and its number there: IP2024-164 is contribution 164 on Indico in 2024 (for 2016, the page in the book; for 2017, the submission number). The ID is its link on this site: #IP2024-164.
  • Placeholders with fewer than 25 words of abstract and double submissions (same title and first author in the same meeting) are dropped, about 25 in all.
  • Formats are reduced to Invited, Oral, Poster and Online; where a meeting did not record the format (the 2016 and 2017 books, part of 2019) it shows as Not recorded.
  • Mini-symposium codes are renumbered every year (MS10 in 2024 is not MS10 in 2025), so a session is always a year and a name.

Nothing on the site is written by an AI: titles, abstracts and sessions are the authors' and organizers' own text, and everything else (topics, map, similar abstracts, statistics) is computed from it.

Embedding

To place abstracts by what they are about, each one becomes a vector: an embedding of its title and abstract (math markup removed), made with bge-m3, the model that did best on the SCA papers in the sibling site. Abstracts with similar vectors are about similar things. In the reading pane, Similar lists the 8 nearest neighbours by cosine similarity, and Same authors lists other abstracts that share an author name.

The map

The embeddings have 1,024 dimensions. UMAP squeezes them to two for the map, keeping each abstract near its closest neighbours. Separately, it reduces them to 10 dimensions, where HDBSCAN finds dense groups: 39 topics of at least 45 abstracts each. Abstracts that fit no group well (1,771, about one in five) are put in the nearest topic and drawn fainter. Each topic's distinctive words come from c-TF-IDF over its titles and abstracts (as in BERTopic); the names were written by hand after reading those words and the titles.

History

The History view lists every meeting since 2009 (place, dates, format, links to its Indico event, book of abstracts and programme), from a hand-made list with a source for each fact. For the meetings with abstracts it adds numbers computed from them: abstracts, sessions, authors, the share of first-time authors, the two-word terms most typical of that year, the largest sessions. The charts show abstracts per meeting by format, returning against new authors, the share of abstracts that mention a theme (hydrogen storage, machine learning, CO2 storage, …) and how the topics' shares changed from year to year. Each chart also has a table of its data.

What is published

Titles, authors and affiliations, abstracts, sessions, formats and links, plus the derived data above. Never e-mail addresses, the books of abstracts or uploaded slides and posters: every entry links to its source on events.interpore.org or interpore.org.

For language models

llms.txt lists the topics with links to one file per topic, and llms-full.txt has every abstract on one line each.

Source

The pipeline (Python, polars, Altair) and the site (plain JavaScript with deck.gl, MiniSearch, KaTeX and Vega-Lite) are on GitHub, with the design notes and the quirks of each source. A sibling site does the same for the papers of the Society of Core Analysts symposium.
